Rent cheaper. Save your deposit.

Rent to Buy lets you rent a new build home at up to 20% below market rent, so you can save for a deposit while you live there.

The basics

What is Rent to Buy?

A government backed scheme for working people who can't afford to buy yet. You rent a new build home from a housing association at no more than 80% of the local market rent, and use the difference to build your deposit.

Your first tenancy is usually up to 2 years and can often be extended. Homes built with government funding stay on Rent to Buy for at least five years, so many providers let you keep saving for longer. Tenancy length varies, so check with the provider.

When you're ready, you can

  • Buy the home outright with a mortgage, using your savings as the deposit. It's sold at market value.
  • Buy a share through Shared Ownership, if your landlord agrees, and increase it over time.
  • Keep renting or move out. Some providers will extend at the same terms. Others will ask you to leave at the end of the tenancy.
Most important thing to know

You are not obliged to buy.

Rent to Buy gives you the opportunity to buy, not the obligation. You won't be penalised for choosing not to.

What does change is whether you can stay. Some housing associations let you keep renting, others need you to move out when the tenancy ends. Get this clarified in writing before you sign.

Where it runs

Not just London. Actually, not London at all.

Rent to Buy runs across England, outside London. In London the equivalent is London Living Rent, which works the same way with its own rules.

Housing associations offer homes in towns and cities across the country, and new ones get added regularly. Homes are often let within days, so set up alerts and check portals weekly.

Areas covered in this guide

  • South East: Kent, Hertfordshire, Guildford
  • East: Cambridge, Chelmsford, Bedford
  • Midlands: Northampton, Milton Keynes and surrounding areas
  • Greater London: London Living Rent instead

Not available in Scotland. Wales has a separate Rent to Own scheme that's closed to new landlords, and Northern Ireland has its own scheme.

Tool 1

Could you be eligible?

A few quick questions based on the most common rules. Each housing association sets its own criteria, so treat this as a starting point.

Common eligibility criteria

  • At least one person in your household is in paid work.
  • You're a first-time buyer. People returning to ownership after a relationship breakdown can also qualify.
  • You can't afford a suitable home to buy where you want to live, usually because you don't have a deposit yet.
  • There's no national income cap for Rent to Buy, but most providers we checked set £80,000 a year. Check with the provider.
  • In London, London Living Rent providers currently list a £75,000 household income cap.
  • A good credit history. CCJs, defaults, IVAs or bankruptcy may rule you out.
  • Some schemes want to see you're actively saving for a deposit.
  • It must be your only home. No subletting and no lodgers.

Being turned down by one provider doesn't mean you'll be turned down by another. Always read each provider's full criteria before applying.

Make it count

Getting the most from your discounted rent

  • Open a Lifetime ISA early if you're 18 to 39 and a first-time buyer. The 12 month clock starts from your first payment.
  • Standing order on rent day. Move the difference between market rent and your rent straight into savings so you never see it.
  • Separate account for your deposit, away from your current account.
  • Track it monthly. Knowing your target and timeline keeps you going.
  • Avoid new debt. Lenders look at your affordability when you apply for a mortgage.
Lifetime ISA, first-time buyers

Up to £1,000 a year, free

  • Open between 18 and 39. Pay in until you're 50.
  • Save up to £4,000 a year and the government adds 25%, up to £1,000 a year. It counts towards your £20,000 ISA allowance.
  • Use it on a first home costing £450,000 or less, bought with a mortgage, at least 12 months after your first payment.
  • Take it out for anything else and there's a 25% charge, so you lose more than the bonus.
  • Offered by lots of banks and investment apps. Compare rates and fees before you pick one.

Heads up: the government plans to replace the Lifetime ISA with a new First Time Buyer ISA. Nothing changes yet for people who already have one. Details and dates haven't been confirmed, so check GOV.UK before you open one.

Know the difference

Rent to Buy vs Shared Ownership

Related, but separate. Plenty of people use Rent to Buy as the stepping stone into Shared Ownership.

Eyes open

Things to be aware of

  • New build homes only. You can't use it on existing homes.
  • Homes are limited and go fast. Set up alerts and check weekly.
  • The discounted period ends. Have a clear plan for what comes next.
  • If you don't buy, you may have to move out at the end of the tenancy. Clarify this upfront.
  • Homes are sold at market value. Get an independent valuation so you know what that really is.
  • Shared Ownership comes with extra costs like service charges and ground rent. Budget for them.
  • Shared Ownership mortgages are different from standard ones. Not every lender offers them, but most major banks do.
  • Rent can rise each year, but it stays capped at 80% of market rent.
FAQ

Questions people ask

Can I apply if I'm self-employed?

Usually, yes. Expect to show one to two years of accounts or tax returns (SA302s) to prove your income. Each housing association has its own requirements.

What if my circumstances change during the tenancy?

Tell your housing association as soon as possible. Job loss, a relationship breakdown or a big income change can affect your tenancy. They'll usually work with you, but be upfront.

Can couples apply together?

Yes. Joint applications are common and both incomes are usually assessed together, so the combined total needs to be within the provider's limit.

Can I buy before my tenancy ends?

Some housing associations allow early purchase, but it's at their discretion. Check your tenancy agreement and ask before you commit.

Is there a minimum deposit to buy at the end?

It depends on the mortgage. For a standard purchase, lenders typically want at least 5% to 10%. For Shared Ownership the deposit is often lower, because you're only buying part of the home.

Does Rent to Buy work in London?

Not under that name. London has London Living Rent instead, where rent is based on a third of local household incomes. Providers currently list a £75,000 household income cap. Share to Buy lists homes.

What's happening to the Lifetime ISA?

The government is planning a new First Time Buyer ISA to replace it. Existing Lifetime ISAs keep working under the current rules for now. No launch date or new figures have been confirmed yet, so check GOV.UK for the latest.
